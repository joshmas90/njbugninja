Tick control for the edges of the yard.

Ticks do not roam the open lawn. They wait in leaf litter, tall grass and low brush along the transition from yard to woods, and on the paths that cross it.

Where ticks are, and where they are not

A tick sits on low vegetation with its front legs out and waits for something warm to brush past. That behavior keeps them in specific places: the border where mowed lawn meets woods or brush, leaf litter, stone walls, wood piles, tall grass, and the shaded paths people and pets take to get from one part of the property to another.

Treating the transition zone

The service is directed at those edges and corridors rather than the whole lawn. On a wooded lot that usually means the perimeter band, the areas around play sets and seating, and the routes between the house and the places you use.

What you can do between visits

  • Keep grass mowed and the yard-to-woods edge clear of leaf litter.
  • Move wood piles, play areas and seating away from the tree line where you can.
  • Check pets and children after time near the edges, especially in spring and early summer.

After the visit

As with every treatment, you get the product-specific re-entry instructions in writing. Follow those for the areas treated.

Tick treatment is not a substitute for checks.

Reducing pressure on a property is worthwhile. It does not replace looking for ticks after time outside, and it does not cover the trail behind the neighborhood.
